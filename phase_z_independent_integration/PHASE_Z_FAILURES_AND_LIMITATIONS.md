## 2. SUMMARY OF FAILED CLAIMS AND METHODOLOGICAL DISCREPANCIES

### Discrepancy 1: Standalone QAOA Does Not Outperform Classical Solvers
- **Claimed in Initial Literature:** QAOA can solve combinatorial routing subproblems better than classical heuristics.
- **Phase Z Finding:** Standalone QAOA without classical incumbent preservation (Proposition 1) produces route distances worse than classical Kernighan-Lin ($578.87\text{ km}$ vs. $542.28\text{ km}$). Furthermore, Kernighan-Lin executes in **1.63 ms**, whereas genuine Qiskit QAOA simulation requires **44.41 ms** ($12.2\times$ slower) and standard QAOA requires **145.64 ms** ($39.9\times$ slower).
- **Scientific Reality:** There is **zero evidence** of standalone quantum supremacy on 16-qubit vehicle bipartitioning subproblems. QAOA's value is strictly catalytic and non-local when embedded within a classical VND metaheuristic.

### Discrepancy 2: The XY Mixer Does Not Preserves Knapsack Capacity Constraints
- **Claimed in Phase Y:** The continuous XY mixer mathematically confines dynamics to the capacity-feasible subspace.
- **Phase Z Finding:** Commutator analysis proves $[H_{\text{XY}}, \sum q_i x_i] \ne 0$ whenever customer parcel demands $q_i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ vary. The XY mixer strictly conserves the cardinality (Hamming weight $\sum x_i = k$), NOT the weighted sum $\sum q_i x_i \le C$.
- **Scientific Reality:** Phase Y's 79.9% feasibility was a statistical artifact of generous capacity slack ($55\%$), not an algebraic conservation law. Classical deterministic capacity repair remains mathematically mandatory.

### Discrepancy 3: The 3.05x Speedup Was Implementation-Driven
- **Claimed in Phase Y:** Algorithmic acceleration delivered a 3.05x reduction in quantum runtime (121.58 ms to 39.84 ms).
- **Phase Z Finding:** Inspection of the Phase Y code revealed that the speedup was generated by halving the sampling iterations (1024 $\to$ 512 shots) and subtracting 70 ms of synthetic hardcoded overhead (`compilation_overhead = 15.0` vs. `85.0`).
- **Scientific Reality:** Both standard and XY-mixer circuits execute in ~44–46 ms under genuine Qiskit 2.4.1 statevector contraction. The speedup was driven by CPU linear algebra shortcuts, not quantum algorithmic innovation.

### Discrepancy 4: The 100% Gating Recall Was a Small-Sample Artifact
- **Claimed in Phase Y:** Policy E achieved 100% recall and avoided 73.3% of quantum calls on a held-out test set ($N=15$).
- **Phase Z Finding:** Phase Y's test set contained exactly 1 positive instance (`inst_750`). Retrieving 1 out of 1 instance yields a trivial 100% metric with no statistical generalizability.
- **Scientific Reality:** Across 20-fold Monte Carlo cross-validation on $N=100$ instances, Policy E achieves a true generalized recall of **$82.7\% \pm 5.4\%$**, an out-of-sample precision of **$36.9\% \pm 3.1\%$**, and avoids **$26.2\% \pm 3.3\%$** of calls.

### Discrepancy 5: Spark 540,000 Records/Sec Was Simulated
- **Claimed in Phase Y:** Apache Spark multi-worker scaling achieved 540,000 records/sec and a 2.96x speedup.
- **Phase Z Finding:** Phase Y ran a NumPy chunking simulation with an empirical formula ($1/w^{0.6} + \text{jvm\_base}$). The Docker daemon was not installed on the host machine.
- **Scientific Reality:** Genuine PySpark 4.2.0 on 4 local cores achieves **87,392 records/sec** at $N=100{,}000$ stops. Furthermore, on 8 MB datasets, in-memory Pandas outperforms Spark by two orders of magnitude due to JVM SerDe overhead.

### Discrepancy 6: Real Heavy-Hex Transpilation Requires Depth 711 (Not 48)
- **Claimed in Phase Y:** Transpiled circuit for IBM Eagle/Heron Heavy-Hex has depth 48 and 36 CZ gates.
- **Phase Z Finding:** An all-to-all dense 16-qubit graph has 120 two-qubit interactions. Routing this across nearest-neighbor Heavy-Hex topology requires SWAP networks, resulting in **558 CZ gates and depth 711**.
- **Scientific Reality:** NISQ gate infidelity ($\sim 0.3\%$ per CZ gate) would reduce unmitigated circuit fidelity to under 19%. Physical execution is currently blocked by missing credentials.
