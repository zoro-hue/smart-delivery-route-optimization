## 1. Catalog of Independently Verified Negative Findings

This deliverable provides an unvarnished summary of all negative results and limitations established by independent reproduction in Phase AC:

### 1. No Demonstrable Quantum Advantage
- Across 60 total boundary subproblem evaluations (30 in Phase AB, 30 held-out in Phase AC), simulated QAOA never achieved a statistically significant route improvement over classical metaheuristics ($p = 0.1020$ vs Tabu Search, $p = 0.6351$ vs Kernighan-Lin, $p = 0.4327$ vs Random Feasible).
- In head-to-head competition, Tabu Search defeated QAOA in **14 of 30 instances** (with 10 ties and only 6 QAOA wins).

### 2. High Quantum Degradation / Rejection Rate (60% to 70%)
- In the Phase AB benchmark, **70.0% of QAOA candidate bipartitions were rejected** by Proposition 1.
- In the Phase AC held-out audit, **50.0% of QAOA candidate bipartitions were rejected**.
- Overall pooled rejection rate is **60.0%**. QAOA candidates frequently degrade the classical incumbent tour because Ising quadratic penalties do not model continuous Euclidean TSP geometry.

### 3. Severe Quantum Simulation Overhead
- On classical CPU hardware, simulating 16-qubit QAOA requires $400\text{ to }645\text{ ms}$, compared to $17\text{ to }34\text{ ms}$ for Tabu Search.
- Classical algorithms are **$19.0\times\text{ to }22.9\times$ faster**.

### 4. Uniform Random Sampling Equivalence
- Generating uniform random bitstrings and applying Proposition 1 + Algorithmic VND achieves **602.90 km** (Phase AB) and **594.63 km** (Phase AC), matching or exceeding QAOA ($606.36\text{ km}$ and $602.16\text{ km}$).
- This confirms that downstream VND local search and Proposition 1 incumbent retention drive solution quality, not quantum superposition or entanglement.

### 5. Single-Machine Spark Execution
- Spark scalability was demonstrated on a single machine under `local[*]` multi-threading, not on a physical multi-node cluster.
- Due to JVM-Python IPC serialization overhead, single-machine in-memory NumPy is $62\times$ faster than PySpark for datasets up to 100,000 stops. Spark's advantage lies in scaling beyond single-machine RAM limits.

### 6. OR-Tools Baseline Labeling Defect
- The "OR-Tools Alone" baseline in Phase AB was generated by a sequential index split (`ta = 1..half`, `tb = half+1..N`), not by invoking the Google OR-Tools constraint solver.
- This produced an inflated baseline distance of **1,307.16 km** and an artificially low latency of **0.04 ms**.
