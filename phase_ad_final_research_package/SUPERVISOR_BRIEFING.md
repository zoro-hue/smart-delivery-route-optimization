## 1. Executive Summary

This briefing summarizes the complete empirical, theoretical, and software outcomes of the ROUTE/IQ Undergraduate Research Opportunity Program (UROP) project across Phases P through AD.

The project successfully constructed and independently audited a cooperative hybrid framework that combines **Apache Spark 4.2.0** for distributed big data processing, **Qiskit 2.4.1 QAOA** for 16-qubit boundary bipartitioning, **Google OR-Tools / Tabu Search / Algorithmic VND** for classical optimization, an **Adaptive Multi-Armed Bandit Controller** for intelligent solver dispatch, and a centralized **Proposition 1 Guardrail** that guarantees 100% capacity feasibility and 0.00% route degradation.

## 2. Research Problem & The Two Computational Pillars

### The Problem
Metropolitan delivery routing ($N \ge 100,000$ deliveries) is intractable for exact solvers, while NISQ quantum hardware cannot directly solve monolithic CVRP due to qubit and coherence limitations.

### Pillar 1: Big Data Distributed Processing (Apache Spark 4.2.0)
- Ingests, schema-validates, and deduplicates customer orders.
- Computes spatial polar angles and centroid projections.
- Reduces metropolitan workloads by **99.984%**, isolating a 16-customer boundary subproblem from 100,000 stops in **3.37 seconds**.

### Pillar 2: Quantum Optimization (QAOA / Qiskit 2.4.1)
- Formulates boundary customer bipartitioning as an Ising QUBO Hamiltonian.
- Investigates Transverse and Subspace XY-Mixers (raising initial feasibility to 89.5%).
- Generates non-local candidate partition cuts via classical CPU statevector simulation.
- Transpiles circuits for IBM Eagle/Heron Heavy-Hex coupling graphs.

## 3. Verified Experimental Findings

Across 30 benchmark instances (240 evaluations) and an independent 30-instance held-out audit:
1. **Classical Heuristics Outperform QAOA:**
   - Tabu Search + VND: **595.45 km** (33.85 ms, 60.0% acceptance)
   - Adaptive Controller + VND: **597.44 km** (34.87 ms, 63.3% acceptance)
   - Fast QAOA + VND: **606.36 km** (644.85 ms, 30.0% acceptance)
   - Tabu Search defeated QAOA in 14 instances, tied in 10, and lost in only 6 ($p = 0.1020$).
2. **Classical Speed Advantage:**
   Classical heuristics ran **$19.05\times\text{ to }22.88\times$ faster** than simulated QAOA on classical CPU hardware.
3. **VND Drives Optimization:**
   Applying Algorithmic VND to initial greedy routing slashes distance by **685.71 km (-52.46%)** in just 8.27 ms.
4. **Guaranteed Operational Reliability:**
   Proposition 1 was tested against 11 adversarial edge cases (capacity boundaries, empty partitions, customer duplicates, omitted nodes, asymmetric directed costs, infinite road edges, and precision drift) with a **100% pass rate (0 failures, 0 bypasses)**. Across all 240 benchmark evaluations, 0 capacity violations and 0 route degradations occurred.
5. **100% Deterministic Reproducibility:**
   All 240 evaluations were reproduced bit-for-bit with **$0.000000\text{ km}$ deviation**. All 57 repository test cases pass.

## 4. Honest Negative Results & Discrepancies Corrected

In accordance with scientific integrity standards, this research package openly reports all negative findings:
1. **No Demonstrated Quantum Advantage:** Simulated QAOA achieved classical parity in route quality, but no statistically significant superiority over Tabu Search or uniform random sampling ($p > 0.05$).
2. **70% Quantum Rejection Rate:** 21 of 30 QAOA candidate bipartitions degraded the route and were rejected by Proposition 1.
3. **Baseline Labeling Defect Corrected:** Config A in Phase AB was generated by a sequential list split, not Google OR-Tools. It has been re-labeled "Trivial Sequential Incumbent" (1,307.16 km / 0.04 ms). The true classical baseline is Config B (OR-Tools + VND: 621.45 km).
4. **Execution Reality Clarification:** Spark scalability was demonstrated on single-machine `local[*]`, not on a distributed multi-node cluster. QAOA was executed via classical CPU statevector simulation, not on a physical QPU.
5. **Software Decoupling:** The cooperative engine is a self-contained research module; it is not yet wired into the production FastAPI backend (`backend/app/main.py`).
