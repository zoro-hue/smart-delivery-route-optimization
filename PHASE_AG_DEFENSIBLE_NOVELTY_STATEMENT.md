## 2. Formal Research Problem Statement

> **Problem Statement:**  
> While hybrid quantum-classical algorithms and Big Data distributed frameworks hold theoretical promise for combinatorial logistics, their practical deployment in Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problems (CVRP) is hindered by three critical challenges:  
> 1. *Scalability and Partitioning:* Upstream geographic customer clustering is rarely coupled smoothly with downstream combinatorial solvers.  
> 2. *NISQ Hardware and Feasibility Limitations:* Gate-based quantum algorithms such as QAOA suffer from limited qubit counts, circuit noise, and high rates of constraint violations (exceeding vehicle capacity or dropping delivery locations).  
> 3. *Operational Risk and Degradation:* Existing hybrid prototypes operate in open-loop configurations without formal safety contracts, meaning experimental quantum steps can degrade feasible routing solutions below standard classical heuristics.  
> 
> There is an unmet need for a unified, fail-safe software architecture that allows enterprise logistics systems to harness Big Data spatial cuts and explore near-term quantum candidate generation without risking classical solution quality or violating fleet capacity constraints.

## 3. Formal Research Objective

> **Research Objective:**  
> To design, implement, and empirically validate an end-to-end, fail-safe cooperative optimization platform (**ROUTE/IQ**) that:  
> 1. Ingests and spatially partitions real-world delivery workloads using distributed Big Data transformations.  
> 2. Establishes a guaranteed feasible baseline using high-performance classical branch-and-bound solvers.  
> 3. Dynamically identifies inter-route boundary subproblems and explores improved bipartitions using an adaptive multi-armed bandit controller that dispatches classical heuristics and simulated QAOA.  
> 4. Enforces a zero-degradation architectural guardrail and universal delivery coverage validation ($R == I$).  
> 5. Evaluates the multi-paradigm system against classical, quantum-inspired, and variational quantum baselines on real-world urban road networks under rigorous resource profiling.

## 4. Five Specific Research Contributions

1. **Closed-Loop Cooperative Hybrid Architecture:**  
   We develop a unified architectural framework that interfaces distributed Big Data workload decomposition (Apache Spark 4.2.0) with a multi-solver candidate pool (OR-Tools, Tabu Search, Kernighan-Lin, and simulated QAOA) and classical Variable Neighborhood Descent (VND), bridging the gap between large-scale data ingestion and combinatorial subproblem optimization.
2. **Centralized Non-Degradation Guardrail & Invariant Enforcement:**  
   We formalize and implement an architectural safety contract guaranteeing that candidate solutions generated by stochastic or variational quantum routines are adopted if and only if they strictly improve total tour cost while strictly satisfying all capacity constraints, eliminating the operational risk of NISQ-induced route degradation:
   $$\text{Cost}(S_{\text{final}}) \le \text{Cost}(S_{\text{incumbent}})$$
3. **Rigorous Delivery Coverage & Immutable Fleet Constraint Engine:**  
   We implement a robust verification and repair operator that guarantees zero dropped stops ($R == I$ multiset equality) and preserves user-specified vehicle counts ($K$) and capacities ($C$), explicitly flagging over-capacity configurations as penalized rather than silently altering fleet parameters.
4. **Adaptive Hyper-Heuristic Strategy Dispatcher:**  
   We implement a contextual multi-armed bandit controller with feature-conditioned gating that dynamically routes subproblems based on remaining computational time budget, search stagnation depth, boundary size, and spatial interaction density.
5. **Comprehensive Multi-Algorithm Empirical Benchmarking & Telemetry:**  
   We present an open, reproducible full-stack benchmarking platform (FastAPI + React 19 / Leaflet GIS) that simultaneously evaluates 8 distinct classical, quantum-inspired, and variational quantum algorithms on identical real-world road networks (NYC GraphML), capturing total distance, latency, CPU time, and memory allocation (RSS and heap).

## 5. Ready-to-Use Journal Paper Contribution Section (LaTeX Format)

*The following text is formatted and directly usable in Section 1 (Introduction) of an academic paper or Capstone thesis:*

```latex
\section{Contributions of this Work}
In this paper, rather than attempting to prove theoretical quantum supremacy or introduce a 
purely isolated routing heuristic, we address the practical systems-engineering challenge of 
integrating Big Data preprocessing and NISQ-era quantum computing into operational vehicle 
routing workflows. Specifically, the primary contributions of this work are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A Closed-Loop Cooperative Routing Architecture:} We propose and implement 
    \textsc{Route/IQ}, a full-stack optimization framework that interfaces distributed spatial 
    polar decomposition (Apache Spark) with classical exact incumbents (Google OR-Tools), an 
    adaptive multi-strategy candidate pool, and Variable Neighborhood Descent (VND) intra-route 
    refinement.
    
    \item \textbf{Monotonic Non-Degradation Guardrail for NISQ Solvers:} We formulate a 
    centralized architectural acceptance guardrail that strictly isolates classical incumbents 
    from the noise, constraint violations, and suboptimality of experimental quantum algorithms 
    (QAOA), ensuring that hybrid exploration never degrades production routing quality.
    
    \item \textbf{Guaranteed Delivery Coverage and Fleet Integrity:} We design an explicit 
    coverage repair and validation engine enforcing strict multiset equality ($R \equiv I$) 
    between input orders and scheduled stops, preventing silent customer omission and strictly 
    respecting user-configured vehicle capacities and fleet limits.
    
    \item \textbf{Contextual Strategy Selection:} We implement an online rule-assisted bandit 
    controller that dynamically balances search exploration and exploitation across Tabu Search, 
    Kernighan-Lin, and simulated QAOA based on real-time computational budgets and graph curvature.
    
    \item \textbf{Empirical Multi-Paradigm Benchmarking on Real Road Networks:} We conduct a 
    rigorous, fair comparative study across eight classical, quantum-inspired, and gate-based 
    quantum algorithms on real-world urban road graphs, transparently documenting solution 
    quality alongside CPU time, RAM usage, and runtime latency to establish honest baseline trade-offs.
\end{enumerate}
```
