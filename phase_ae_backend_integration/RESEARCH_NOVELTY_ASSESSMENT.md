## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemic Stance

A critical objective of this assessment is to establish **intellectual honesty and scientific precision**. In modern quantum computing and operations research literature, claims of "quantum supremacy" or "quantum advantage" on NISQ-era hardware for NP-hard routing problems (such as CVRP) are frequently overstated or methodologically flawed due to weak classical baselines, unfair graph metrics, or cherry-picked subproblems.

ROUTE/IQ explicitly rejects unwarranted quantum advantage claims. Rigorous experimental ablation conducted across Phase AA, Phase AB, Phase AC, and Phase AE demonstrates that:
1. Standalone QAOA executed on current statevector simulators or NISQ architectures does **not** outperform state-of-the-art classical metaheuristics (such as Google OR-Tools or Guided Local Search) on CVRP.
2. The genuine scientific and methodological contribution of ROUTE/IQ lies in the **Cooperative Architecture with Proposition 1 Non-Degradation Guardrail**, the **Centroid Polar-Angle Spatial Decomposition**, the **Adaptive Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB UCB-1) Controller**, and the **Deterministic Coverage Invariant ($R == I$)**.
3. QAOA serves as an exploratory boundary-permutation candidate generator within a hybrid portfolio. If QAOA (or any solver) produces an inferior or infeasible candidate, Proposition 1 deterministically rejects it, preserving the classical incumbent.

## 3. Detailed Novelty Breakdown by Category

### Category A: Genuinely Novel Methodological Contribution
- **The Proposition 1 Non-Degradation Guardrail & Hybrid Cooperative Contract:**
  In classical-quantum operations research, researchers typically benchmark a quantum algorithm in isolation against a classical algorithm. If the quantum algorithm performs poorly (as QAOA frequently does on constrained VRPs), the paper concludes either negative results or claims "future fault-tolerant hardware will fix this."
  ROUTE/IQ contributes a **fail-safe cooperative paradigm**: quantum computing is treated as an exploratory subproblem generator within a bounded contractual optimization loop. Because Proposition 1 guarantees $C(S_{\text{coop}}) \le C(S_{\text{incumbent}})$, the system possesses a mathematical guarantee that experimental quantum algorithms can be introduced into production environments with **zero risk of operational degradation**.

### Category B: Potential & Experimentally Demonstrated Methodological Contributions
1. **Centroid Polar-Angle Spatial Partitioning with Boundary Extraction for Quantum Embedding:**
   Decomposing a large-scale real-world road graph (54,128 nodes in NYC) via Apache Spark into angular wedges and extracting only the boundary interface vertices for QUBO/QAOA formulation directly addresses the fundamental bottleneck of NISQ hardware: limited qubit counts.
2. **Adaptive Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB UCB-1) Controller across Hybrid Solver Portfolios:**
   Dynamically allocating search effort between quantum variational heuristics and classical local search operators based on real-time boundary improvement deltas.
3. **Universal Fleet Coverage Verification ($R == I$):**
   A rigorous post-processing and validation invariant ensuring that subproblem-based hybrid routing algorithms cannot achieve low objective values by dropping difficult or distant customer locations.

### Category C: Engineering Integrations & Adaptations
1. **Apache Spark 4.2.0 + Qiskit 2.4.1 + Google OR-Tools 9.15 Pipeline:**
   Seamless interoperability running on Python 3.14.2, coordinating distributed big data abstractions with quantum circuit statevector simulation and C++ constraint programming bindings.
2. **Real-World Road Network Snapping & Waypoint Reconstruction:**
   Projecting arbitrary GPS customer coordinates onto the OpenStreetMap Manhattan/NYC road graph via `road_network_service.py` using Dijkstra shortest paths, producing turn-by-turn driving geometries instead of naive Euclidean straight lines.
3. **Multi-Faceted Resource Profiling:**
   Simultaneous acquisition of resident set size (`psutil`), heap allocation (`tracemalloc`), user/system CPU time (`time.process_time`), and monotonic wall-clock latency (`time.perf_counter`).
