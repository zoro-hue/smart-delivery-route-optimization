## 1. Principles of Scientific Fairness

A major finding of Phase AA was that experimental designs comparing 512 quantum samples against a single classical run introduce severe sampling bias. To eliminate all confounding factors, Phase AB enforces strict experimental standardization across all candidate generators:

1. **Standardized Evaluation Budget:**
   Every generator operates with an identical candidate budget parameter:
   $$B = 512 \text{ candidate solutions evaluated per subproblem}$$
2. **Identical Downstream Post-Processing:**
   Every candidate generated by any method is subjected to the exact same:
   - Nearest-Neighbor tour reconstruction sequence.
   - Algorithmic Variable Neighborhood Descent (2-Opt, Relocate, Swap, Cross-Exchange).
   - Centralized Proposition 1 Guardrail acceptance test.
3. **Decoupled Sampling and Repair Accounting:**
   Each generator separately reports raw sampling time, capacity repair time, unique bitstring count, and feasible fraction.
4. **Shared Seed Control:**
   Every randomized generator (Random, MS-KL, Tabu, QAOA) uses a shared seeded PRNG `np.random.RandomState(seed)` for exact reproducibility across runs.

## 3. Analysis of Candidate Quality & Diversity

1. **The Sampling Diversity Paradox:**
   - Uniform Random sampling generates the highest number of unique bitstrings ($480.2 / 512$), but lacks objective guidance, resulting in higher final distance ($602.90\text{ km}$).
   - Fast QAOA generates fewer unique bitstrings ($14.2 / 512$) concentrated around the low-energy eigenspaces of $H_C$, but 70% of its accepted solutions are identical to or slightly worse than classical greedy partitions.
   - Tabu Search achieves the optimal balance: it generates structured, memory-guided trajectory diversity ($195.4$ unique solutions), achieving the best final distance (**595.45 km**) and highest acceptance rate (**60.0%**).
